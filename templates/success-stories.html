From Orangi to Harvard — Dreams Know No Limits

One day, as a kid, you're wondering if you will ever go to school. Next thing you know, you are packing your bags to go to Harvard!

Kainat Ansari, a TCF Alumna from Orangi Town, was accepted to the Ivy League Harvard Graduate School of Education. Coming from Orangi — a small town, a slum — women like me are told places like Harvard aren't for us. Getting in was beyond what my parents or I ever imagined.

It feels like just yesterday that Kainat walked through TCF's gates with her father for her brother's admission. She saw girls playing badminton, books in the library, classrooms alive with ideas she couldn't yet grasp. She knew she belonged there — and pleaded with her father for a chance.

And when she finally got it, she worked harder than ever to catch up. Soon, she rose to the top of her class. We celebrate Kainat's grit and your belief in the potential of Pakistan's children. Change truly begins with education.

Kainat, you make us proud. Keep flying high!

Fruit-seller's Son, GIKI Graduate

The repairing of our home can wait — but this opportunity might not come again.

The sweet scent of mangoes fills the air as we enter a small, mud-brick home in a dusty street of Khuda ki Basti, Karachi. Muhammad Ahmad, a fruit-seller, stacking up mangoes on his pushcart, smiling from ear to ear.

"With my earnings, it would not have been possible for me to put Asad through a good school, college and university." He had to drop out of school at a young age to support his family. "It is my dream to educate all my children and TCF is making it possible."

In their community, boys are expected to take up full-time jobs at a young age — but Baba encouraged Asad to focus on his education. When Asad was leaving for university, his father handed him money he had been saving to get their roof fixed. "The repairing of our home can wait but this opportunity might not come again."

Asad graduated from the prestigious Ghulam Ishaq Khan Institute of Engineering Sciences and Technology and is ready to begin his first job at a software company. Two of his younger siblings are currently first-year students at TCF College while two other sisters study at TCF school — all with big dreams of their own!

A Farmer's Son Will Become Anything He Dreams Of

Whatever I am today is because of my father. He is my hero!

Every morning, Abdul Wahid's father would drop him and his brother off to school on his bicycle before heading out to the fields — labouring under the scorching heat of the sun to provide for his family.

As his father got older, many villagers suggested he pull his sons out of school and put them to work. "What's the use of sending them to school? A farmer's son will become a farmer anyway." But Ahmad disregarded their comments. "You can be anything you want if you work hard," he told his children.

Today, Abdul Wahid pursues a Bachelor of Science in Physics on a full scholarship at LUMS — the first child in his village in Bhurewala, Punjab to attend university.

"I can never forget the moment when I told him about my admission. He was in tears — for he knew his hard work and struggle had finally paid off." He chose our future over a comfortable life. He is my hero!

TCF Alumna Iram, a Policewoman #ChoseToChallenge!

These days will pass once we are educated and on our feet.

"A policewoman? What kind of a job is that for a girl?" — Iram's relatives would ask her mother. At the time, TCF alumna Iram from Kalabagh, Punjab was training to become a patrol officer with the National Highways and Motorway Police — an occupation generally considered unconventional for women.

Raised by a single mother who worked day and night as a tailor and domestic help, Iram and her sisters found strength in her words as others told them it was a waste of money to educate girls.

The day Iram learned she had aced her training and was appointed Junior Patrol Officer, she heard from her father after nearly nine years. He called to tell her that he was proud of her. Today, the same people who used to mock her mother now tell their daughters to follow in her footsteps.

"Like in any other field, the presence of women in Motorway Police is equally important. There are so many female passengers that call out for help and it is important that there are women here who can help them."

On IWD2021, we are celebrating the passion, excellence and strength of women who #ChooseToChallenge.

TCF Alumna · Harvard Summer Program Grand Prize Winner

From Humble to Harvard

Education has empowered me. Now, I'm doing something I always wanted to do — helping people around me.

Anum Fatima started her remarkable journey with TCF in seventh grade. Born in a family facing significant financial challenges, Anum saw her father working tirelessly as a driver. From a very young age, she committed herself to his only expectation — work hard and focus on your studies.

After graduating from TCF in 2006, Anum went on to become the first girl in her family to attend college and completed her MBA from College of Business Management in Karachi. In 2013, she received a scholarship to attend a Harvard University summer programme — where she was selected as the Grand Prize Winner in her class.

During a subsequent internship in Washington DC, Anum designed a micro-lending project for her community and has already provided micro loans to families, helping them improve their own lives.

"I'm a bridge between people who want to help and the ones who need help. I get enormous satisfaction from knowing that education has empowered me."
